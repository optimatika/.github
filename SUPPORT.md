# Support

## How do I use this?

Ask in Discussions on the repository concerned. Search the existing threads first, since
questions about modelling, solver selection and numerical tolerances tend to recur and
many already have answers. Documentation and articles are at
[ojalgo.org](https://www.ojalgo.org/), and the wiki holds the longer worked examples.

## Something is broken

Open an issue on the repository concerned. Say which version you are on, what you
expected, and what happened instead, and include the smallest program that shows it. A
model that actually fails is worth far more than a description of one.

## Both of those are public

Whatever you post — and whatever it reveals about what you are building — is visible to
everyone, and stays that way.

Answers come from whoever happens to know one and has the time: a maintainer, another
user, or nobody. There is no response-time commitment. This is open source under the MIT
licence, and the work behind it is volunteered.

## Something looks like a security problem

Do not open an issue. Follow the [security policy](SECURITY.md) instead.

## Commercial support

[Optimatika](https://www.optimatika.se/) offers paid support to organisations whose code
depends on ojAlgo: help with modelling, performance work, and direct access to the people
who develop the library. It is an agreement about expertise rather than a transfer of
risk, and it comes with no warranty.

Commercial support buys attention, prioritisation and commitment. It does not buy
permission — you already have that.

Details and enquiries at [optimatika.se](https://www.optimatika.se/contact/).

## The licence is not affected

ojAlgo stays open source under the MIT licence. There is no commercial edition and no
dual licence. Paid support changes nothing about the terms you already have.
